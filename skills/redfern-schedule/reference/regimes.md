# Governing regimes

The default is the IBA Rules on the Taking of Evidence (2020). The other regimes are selectable variants. IBA 2020 sets the substantive admissibility tests. ICC, LCIA, and ICSID change the procedural framing and commonly borrow the IBA tests as the substantive standard. Prague does not raise the relevance-and-materiality bar so much as change the whole posture: production is discouraged and tribunal-routed, the unit is a single specific document rather than a category, the timing is fixed to the case-management conference, and it adds a public-domain filter.

## IBA 2020 (default)

The adversarial, party-driven model. Requests run party to party. The unit is a single document or a narrow and specific category. Apply `iba-3-3-checklist.md` and `iba-9-2-objections.md` as written. Article 3 governs requests to produce. Article 9 governs admissibility and exclusion.

## Prague 2018

The inquisitorial, tribunal-driven model under the Rules on the Efficient Conduct of Proceedings in International Arbitration (Prague Rules), Article 4. When the regime is Prague, change behaviour:

- **Unit.** A single specific document per row, not a category. Disable category-style requests.
- **Default posture.** Production is discouraged (Article 4.2). Show a clear note at the top of the schedule: production under the Prague Rules is the exception, not the norm, and is routed through the tribunal.
- **Three hard gates (Article 4.5).** A request must be:
  1. relevant and material to the outcome,
  2. not in the public domain,
  3. in the other party's possession or control.
  All three are required. The public-domain filter (2) has no direct IBA equivalent, and the single-document unit is stricter than the IBA category, so a borderline category request that would pass IBA 3.3 can fail under Prague.
- **Timing (Article 4.3 and 4.4).** The request should be raised at the case-management conference. A later request is exceptional and needs a reason the party could not have asked earlier. Add a CMC-timing field and flag a late request.
- **Decision column.** Reframe toward a tribunal-orders posture (Article 4.6: the tribunal, after hearing the party's view, may order production). The tribunal may also, on its own initiative, request a party to submit documents under its proactive-role power (Article 3.2(a)), which is a separate provision from the Article 4 request mechanism.

## ICC

The ICC Rules do not contain an express document-production article. The tribunal establishes the facts by all appropriate means (**Article 25** in the 2021 Rules, renumbered **Article 26** in the 2026 Rules, in force 1 June 2026. In the 2026 Rules Article 25 instead covers New Claims and Article 28 covers Closing of the Proceedings, so do not cite either for fact-finding). Production is an implied power. The ICC Note to Parties and Arbitral Tribunals encourages Redfern Schedules and scope control, and **Appendix IV (Case Management Techniques), item (d)** lists ways to limit production, including item (d)(v), "using a schedule of document production to facilitate the resolution of issues," which is the Redfern Schedule. Use the IBA 3.3 and 9.2 tests as the substantive standard. Add the ICC case-management framing: keep requests tied to what is genuinely necessary, and note the tribunal's scope-control role. Cite Article 25 (2021 Rules) or Article 26 (2026 Rules) depending on which version the arbitration runs under.

## LCIA

The LCIA Arbitration Rules 2020, Article 22.1(v), give the tribunal power to order a party to produce documents in its possession, custody, or power which the tribunal decides to be relevant. The LCIA default leans against broad common-law disclosure and tracks the IBA relevance-and-materiality language. Use the IBA 3.3 and 9.2 tests as the substantive standard. Add the LCIA framing: production is a tribunal-discretion power over identified, relevant, material documents, not broad disclosure.

## ICSID

The ICSID Arbitration Rules 2022 govern ICSID Convention arbitrations. There is no automatic right to disclosure. Rule 36 (Evidence: General Principles) sets the tribunal's power over evidence, and Rule 36(3) lets the tribunal call upon a party to produce documents at any stage. Rule 37 (Disputes Arising from Requests for Production of Documents) governs production disputes: where the parties disagree about producing a document or category, the tribunal shall consider all relevant circumstances including the scope and timeliness of the request, the relevance and materiality of the documents requested, the burden of production, and the basis of the objection. In practice ICSID tribunals organise these disputes on a Redfern Schedule and draw on the IBA Rules for the substantive standard, so use the IBA 3.3 and 9.2 tests here too. Add the ICSID framing: production is tribunal-controlled and discretionary, the Rule 37 factors mirror the IBA tests, and an investor-State posture often brings the political and institutional sensitivity question into play (apply the content-based (f) prompt in `iba-9-2-objections.md`).

## Picking the regime in practice

If the user does not know the regime, default to IBA 2020 and say so. The IBA Rules apply only where the parties or the tribunal adopt them, but they are widely used and tribunals often draw on them for guidance even where the institutional rules are silent, so they are a reasonable working default for this skill when no regime is specified. Confirm what actually governs. If the arbitration agreement or Procedural Order No. 1 names a regime, use that.
