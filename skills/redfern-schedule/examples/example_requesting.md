# Example: requesting party, first draft

The requesting party drafts the schedule, runs the Article 3.3 pre-flight, and gets the internal flags memo. Fact pattern and known-good answer in `gold-fact-pattern.md`.

## Input

- Role: requesting (Helios Power Ltd, Claimant).
- Regime: IBA 2020.
- Round: first draft.
- State party: the Republic of Marateca and Marateca National Grid are marked State or state-owned.
- Issues list: provided (Issues 1, 2, 3).
- Requests: R1 to R4 as listed in the fact pattern.

The privilege gate fired first and the user confirmed. The interview was skipped because the trigger carried role, regime, round, the state-party marker, and the issues list.

### Internal flags memo (privileged work product, do not send)

- **R3 is weak under Article 3.3.**
  - Gate A (identification): fail. "All documents concerning the Project" is a topic, not a narrow and specific category. No date range, no named custodian, no document type. This reads as a fishing expedition and is exposed to an Article 9.2(c) burden objection.
  - Gate B (relevance and materiality): fail. The relevance line is boilerplate, ties to no pleaded issue, and does not articulate materiality to the outcome. The issue matcher returns untied.
  - Gate C (possession): fail. No statement that the documents are outside the Claimant's possession, and the sweep would capture documents the Claimant already holds.
  - Recommendation: narrow to a defined custodian, subject, document type, and date range tied to a specific issue, or drop it. Narrowed, R3 would look like R5.
- R1, R2, R4, and R5 pass all three gates. R5 is the electronic request: it passes Gate A on identification (named custodians, defined subject, date range) and is strong on efficiency because it supplies search terms, so it carries no 9.2(g) proportionality risk. The weak counterfactual, "all emails of MNG staff concerning the Project", would fail Gate A and invite a 9.2(g) objection. No action needed beyond the user's own review of the drafting.

**Calibration note.** Run for the requesting role under IBA 2020 with the issues list provided. R1, R2, and R4 are tied to pleaded issues. R3 is untied and flagged. Drafting quality and the strength of the relevance case are for counsel, not validated here.

## What this example demonstrates

- The 3.3 pre-flight passing four requests and failing one, with the reason per gate.
- The flags memo isolating the weak request and saying exactly why, without rewriting it.
- The issue matcher tying R1, R2, R4, and R5 to pleaded issues and returning R3 untied.
- The electronic request (R5) shown in its strong form, with custodians, a date window, and search terms, beside its weak counterfactual.
- The requesting role writing only the No., Document(s) or Category Requested, and Relevance and Materiality columns, leaving Objections, Reply, and Tribunal's Decision blank.
