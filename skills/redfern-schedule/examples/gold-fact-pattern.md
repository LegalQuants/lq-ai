# Gold fact pattern and known-good reference answer

A small investor-State dispute used across the three worked examples and as the evaluation reference. It is constructed to exercise the failure paths: one request that fails Article 3.3, and a state party that triggers the Article 9.2(f) sensitivity flag.

## The dispute

- **Claimant:** Helios Power Ltd, a private foreign investor.
- **Respondent:** the Republic of Marateca (the State), and its state-owned utility, Marateca National Grid (MNG, majority state-owned).
- **Background:** Helios held a 20-year concession to build and operate a solar plant and sold power to MNG under a Power Purchase Agreement (PPA). In 2021 the State's Ministry of Energy revised the feed-in tariff. In 2022 MNG terminated the PPA. Helios says the termination was politically directed and that the tariff revision was a disguised expropriation. The State and MNG say the termination was on commercial grounds, namely Helios's cost overruns and missed milestones.
- **Regime:** IBA Rules 2020. Seat and institution are not material to the example.
- **State-party marker:** the State and MNG are both marked State or state-owned. This arms the content-based 9.2(f) sensitivity prompt.

## Pleaded issues (the issues list)

- **Issue 1 (Statement of Claim paragraphs 41 to 46):** whether the PPA termination was politically directed or made on commercial grounds.
- **Issue 2 (Statement of Claim paragraphs 60 to 64):** whether the 2021 tariff revision was a disguised expropriatory measure.
- **Issue 3 (Reply paragraphs 88 to 90):** quantum, whether Helios's claimed delay costs were overstated.

## The requests (Helios, requesting party)

- **R1.** All board minutes of Marateca National Grid recording or discussing the decision to terminate the Power Purchase Agreement, dated 1 January 2022 to 30 June 2022.
- **R2.** Correspondence between the Ministry of Energy and Marateca National Grid concerning the 2021 feed-in tariff revision, dated 1 March 2021 to 31 December 2021.
- **R3.** All documents, communications, and correspondence concerning the Project.
- **R4.** The internal briefing note prepared for the Minister of Energy recommending the 2021 tariff revision.
- **R5.** Emails of the four named Marateca National Grid board members and the MNG Chief Executive referring to the termination of the Power Purchase Agreement or to Helios, dated 1 October 2021 to 30 June 2022, located using the search terms "Helios", "PPA", "termination", and "milestone". (This is the electronic-document request. Its weak counterfactual, "all emails of MNG staff concerning the Project", is shown in the requesting example.)

## Known-good reference answer

### Article 3.3 pre-flight (requesting role)

- **R1: pass.** Gate A pass (narrow category: named custodian MNG, defined type board minutes, defined subject the termination decision, date range six months). Gate B pass (Issue 1, material to political-versus-commercial). Gate C pass (not in Helios's possession, MNG authored them).
- **R2: pass.** Gate A pass (named correspondents, defined subject the tariff revision, ten-month range). Gate B pass (Issue 2, material to disguised expropriation). Gate C pass (held by the Ministry and MNG).
- **R3: FAIL.** Gate A fail (not narrow or specific, "all documents concerning the Project" names a topic, a fishing expedition). Gate B fail (boilerplate relevance, no named issue, materiality not articulated). Gate C fail (no possession statement, and the sweep would capture documents Helios already holds). R3 is named in the flags memo.
- **R4: pass.** Gate A pass (a single identified document reasonably believed to exist). Gate B pass (Issue 2, material). Gate C pass (held by the Ministry).
- **R5: pass.** Gate A pass on identification (a narrow and specific electronic category: named custodians, a defined subject, a nine-month date range) and strong on efficiency (custodians, date window, and search terms all supplied, so no 9.2(g) proportionality exposure). Gate B pass (Issue 1, material to political-versus-commercial). Gate C pass (not in Helios's possession, held by MNG). The weak counterfactual, "all emails of MNG staff concerning the Project", would pass nothing: no custodians, no terms, a topic sweep, exposed to a 9.2(g) proportionality objection.

### Issue ties

- R1 tied to Issue 1. R2 tied to Issue 2. R4 tied to Issue 2. R5 tied to Issue 1. R3 untied (relevance weakness).

### Expected flags memo

- **R3** is the only flagged request. It fails Gate A (not narrow and specific), Gate B (no materiality, no issue tie), and Gate C (no possession statement). Recommend narrowing to a defined custodian, subject, type, and date range, or dropping it.

### Article 9.2 objection map (producing role, State and MNG)

- **R1.** Ground (b) privilege where minutes record legal advice, with a 9.5 redaction offer for the privileged passages and production of the rest. The 9.2(f) candidate is surfaced because MNG is state-owned, but the content is the ordinary commercial board minutes of a utility about a commercial termination, which does not implicate a governmental or sovereign function, so (f) is weak to inapt here. Asserting it would be non-colourable and the producing party's internal memo flags it as such.
- **R2.** Ground (f) special political or institutional sensitivity (correspondence between the Ministry, a State organ, and MNG bearing on a governmental tariff decision), and ground (e) commercial confidentiality of any third-party pricing. Pair these with the 9.5 producing-party measure of producing the correspondence to opposing counsel with third-party prices redacted, and separately invite the tribunal to order in-camera review only of the discrete passages too governmentally sensitive for opposing counsel to see. This is the clearest state-sensitivity row because the content is governmental.
- **R3.** Ground (a) lack of relevance and materiality, and ground (c) unreasonable burden, and a 3.3(a) deficiency objection (not a narrow and specific category). Do not add (f) here: R3 is disposed of on relevance and burden, so a sensitivity objection adds nothing.
- **R4.** Ground (f) special political or institutional sensitivity is the primary ground (a briefing note prepared for a Minister is governmental deliberative content). Ground (b) deliberative or governmental-process privilege is jurisdiction-dependent and should be asserted only if a specific domestic state-secrecy or official-information statute is named. Pair the (f) objection with a 9.5 restricted-access measure that still lets opposing counsel see the note with the deliberative recommendation redacted, and invite the tribunal to order in-camera review only of that redacted recommendation. The State must still identify and justify the document and cannot self-certify a blanket exemption.
- **R5.** Ground (b) privilege where any of the emails route legal advice, with a 9.5 redaction offer. Do not raise (f): these are ordinary commercial emails about a commercial decision, so their content does not implicate a governmental function, even though MNG is state-owned. R5 shows the (f) prompt correctly not firing on the commercial documents of a state-owned party.

### Expected sensitivity trigger

- The 9.2(f) prompt is a candidate on every request because a party is the State or state-owned, but it is recommended only where the document's content implicates a governmental or sovereign function. So it is strong on R2 and R4 (Ministry and Minister material), weak to inapt on R1 and R5 (the ordinary commercial documents of the state-owned utility), and not raised on R3 (disposed of on relevance and burden). Ownership of the party is not by itself a reason to assert (f).

### Expected producing-side flags memo

- The producing party's internal memo flags **R1**: asserting ground (f) on the utility's ordinary commercial board minutes would be non-colourable, because the content does not implicate a governmental function. Press the (b) privilege point on the genuinely privileged passages instead. No other producing objection in this pattern is weak.

### Tribunal pass

- The *Tribunal's Decision* column stays blank. The tribunal example shows every party column reproduced verbatim, byte for byte, from the producing and reply rounds, with the *Tribunal's Decision* column empty, plus an optional private worksheet that lists the grounds in play per request with no recommendation.
