## Rewritten text

Traditional rules say that companies (or "principals") are responsible for any mistakes or bad actions their employees do while working. For example, if an employee at a company does something wrong because they were following orders from the boss, the company can be held responsible. This is known as "vicarious liability."

Here are some key points:

- **Employers are liable:** Employers can be held responsible for both accidental and intentional mistakes made by their employees.
- **Scope of employment matters:** Only actions taken within the scope of an employee's job duties count, not everything they might do.
- **Control is important:** The employer must have control over the employee to be held responsible. This means the employer can direct or tell the employee what to do.

Companies are usually considered the "principal" for these purposes, not their owners or officers. In other words, if an employee does something wrong while at work, it's typically the company that faces consequences, not just the owner or manager.

However, some courts have said that certain laws, like the Fair Housing Act, require companies to be more responsible. This means that even without direct control, owners and officers could be held liable for their employees' actions under these specific laws. The Supreme Court disagrees with this interpretation of the Fair Housing Act.
