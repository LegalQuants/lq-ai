# Comms Improver: Privacy Policy — Data Sharing and Retention Clauses

**Audience:** Sales team — what to tell prospects about how customer data is handled; when to escalate
**Purpose:** Equip sales reps to accurately answer prospect questions about data sharing and retention without overpromising or misrepresenting
**Length constraint:** Matched to original; expanded slightly for actionability
**Tone:** Practical and direct — yes/no boundaries with escalation guidance

---

## Rewritten text

> **What you can tell prospects about how we handle their data**
>
> **On sharing customer data:**
>
> - ✅ You can say: "We don't sell or trade your data. We don't share it with third parties for their marketing purposes."
> - ✅ You can say: "We only share your data in limited circumstances described in our Privacy Policy."
> - ❌ Don't say: "We never share your data with anyone." That's not accurate — the Privacy Policy allows sharing in certain situations (for example, with vendors who help us run the service). The key point is we don't share it for others to market to you.
>
> **On how long we keep customer data:**
>
> - ✅ You can say: "We keep your data only as long as we need it for the purpose we collected it — after that, we don't hold on to it longer than required."
> - ❌ Don't use specific timeframes (like "we delete your data after 12 months") unless Legal has confirmed the exact number for the specific data type in question — retention periods vary by data type.
> - 🔼 Escalate to Legal if a prospect asks: "How long exactly do you keep [specific type of data]?" — the answer depends on the type of data and we want to be precise.
>
> **When to escalate to Legal:**
> Escalate any time a prospect asks a question you can't answer from the bullets above. Common triggers:
> - "What third parties do you share our data with?"
> - "Can you tell me exactly what's in your Privacy Policy?"
> - "We have specific contractual requirements around data retention — can you accommodate those?"
> - Anything that starts with "our legal/compliance team wants to know…"
>
> When in doubt, the right answer to a prospect is: "Great question — let me get our legal team to respond directly so you have an accurate answer."

## What was changed

- **Led with what sales reps actually need:** clear yes/no/escalate structure instead of policy prose. The original text describes the company's legal obligations; the rewrite translates those into talking points.
- **Active, second-person framing:** "you can say / don't say" replaces abstract declarations ("we do not sell, trade, or otherwise transfer").
- **Retention vagueness surfaced explicitly:** the original retention clause is deliberately open-ended ("depends on the type of data and the purpose"). Rather than paper over this with a false summary, the rewrite makes the vagueness visible and flags it as an escalation trigger — this protects reps from inadvertently overpromising.
- **Escalation triggers named explicitly:** the original gives no guidance on when reps should stop and hand off; the rewrite fills that gap with concrete examples.

## Preservation-of-meaning concerns

**One concern worth flagging:** The original says the company does not share personal information *except in accordance with this Privacy Policy.* That exception matters — it means sharing *does* occur in circumstances the Policy describes (e.g., with service providers, in connection with a business transfer, or as required by law). The rewrite flags this directly in the ❌ "don't say" bullet to prevent reps from inadvertently telling prospects "we never share your data with anyone," which would be inaccurate and could create a misrepresentation problem. Verify that this characterization of the permitted-sharing scope is accurate before distributing.

**Secondary concern:** Retention language in the original is genuinely vague by design — it preserves flexibility. The rewrite treats this as a feature, not a bug, and turns it into an escalation trigger. If the company does have specific retention schedules for common data types that it's comfortable disclosing to prospects, Legal could provide reps a short reference sheet to sit alongside this guidance.

## Suggested follow-ups

1. **If a prospect pushes back on vague retention language:** have Legal prepare a one-page "data retention overview" for common data types that sales can share with prospect legal/compliance teams — this is a frequent procurement question that benefits from a prepared answer.
2. **If reps ask "what *are* the circumstances where we do share data?":** that's a good question for a follow-up internal FAQ — the Privacy Policy lists them, but reps may benefit from a plain-language summary of permitted sharing categories (e.g., "our vendors who help us run the service," "if required by law").
3. **If this text will be shared with international prospects:** flag to Legal that GDPR and CCPA impose specific disclosure obligations that may require more precise language than this summary provides.
