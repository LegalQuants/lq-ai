LegalQuants states its own membership routes at [legalquants.com/brand](https://legalquants.com/brand), not here — that page, not this one, is the canonical description if the two ever disagree. As LegalQuants states it: there are three routes in — completing the LQ Residency, passing LQ Assess, or receiving a direct invitation. There is no paid tier and no purchasable credential; membership is maintained by contribution rather than payment, and members who stop contributing for an extended period are removed to make room for more active ones.

Contributing to LQ.AI does not replace those three routes. It is one of the ways contribution itself is demonstrated once someone is already inside the community, or is being evaluated for one of the three routes in. The build brief for this documentation site — the docs-site mini-PRD (PR #511) — names contributors, including their coding agents, as people "for whom a material contribution is a recognised route into LegalQuants membership." That is the extent of what this repository's own record supports: a material contribution counts, on the same terms as any other form of active participation the brand page describes — in its words, "taking part in LQAI or other LQ Labs build initiatives, contributing to LQ Assess benchmark tasks, and generally advancing LQ's mission". Nothing in this repository defines a threshold for "material," a fast-track, or a guarantee — the decision sits with the people who run the three routes, not with this page.

What counts as a contribution worth showing is not a mystery, though. [The contribution board](board.md) lists specific, scoped, mergeable items with a contributor profile and an effort estimate attached to each; a merged pull request against one of them, a certified skill acceptance pack, or a reviewed and merged skill are all concrete, checkable evidence of the kind the brand page's criteria describe. A single typo fix is real, but it is not the same order of evidence as one of those.
